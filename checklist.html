The SBIR Phase II
Preparation Checklist

47 items that separate awarded Phase II contracts from stalled ones. Built from DCAA public audit findings, FAR Part 31 cost principles, and the specific failure patterns we see across SBIR teams entering cost-reimbursable contracting for the first time.

Why This Checklist Exists

Phase I awards are firm-fixed-price, so DCAA rarely audits your accounting system directly. Phase II is different. Phase II awards are cost-reimbursable, which triggers a Standard Form 1408 pre-award accounting system survey before funds are obligated. The #1 DCAA audit finding — across every contractor size — is timekeeping noncompliance. This checklist is organized around the 13 SF-1408 criteria plus the operational prep work most SBIR teams discover too late.

01Timekeeping System9 items

SF-1408 Criterion 2e evaluates whether your timekeeping system identifies each employee's labor by cost objective on a daily basis. This is the single most common failure point for SBIR teams transitioning to Phase II. Most teams have software that could be compliant but practices that aren't.

  • All employees record labor daily — not weekly, not retrospectively. DCAA expects contemporaneous entry at the time of work.
  • Every entry identifies the contract or CLIN the labor was performed for. Free-text descriptions without contract attribution do not satisfy the requirement.
  • Timestamped audit trail — the system records when each entry was created, not just the date the work was performed.
  • No gaps longer than 3 business days in any employee's record over the last 90 days.
  • Entries are reviewed and approved by a named supervisor or program manager on at least a weekly cadence.
  • Corrections are tracked separately from original entries, with reason codes and an audit log.
  • The CEO and all executives record labor — no exemptions. DCAA has no tolerance for "my time doesn't need to be tracked" from anyone on payroll.
  • Part-time and contract employees recording labor use the same system and standards as full-time staff.
  • Written timekeeping policy exists as a named document (e.g., SOP-FIN-001) that can be produced on request.
Common Failure

Teams deploy a DCAA-compliant timekeeping product, train engineers, and stop enforcing use after two weeks. At Phase II application time, the system contains sporadic entries with multi-week gaps. Backfilling retroactively creates records with the same timestamp as today — a signal auditors catch immediately.

02Labor Distribution & CLIN Mapping6 items

SF-1408 Criteria 2b, 2f, and 2i. Every direct cost must be traceable to the specific contract and CLIN it was incurred for. Multi-CLIN contracts compound this — you need CLIN-level discipline, not just contract-level.

  • Direct labor accounts are separate from indirect labor accounts in your general ledger.
  • Each active contract has a dedicated job code or project code that flows from labor entry through to the GL.
  • CLIN selection happens at labor entry — not at invoice time. Engineers pick from a defined list of active CLINs at the moment they log work.
  • Monthly reconciliation of timekeeping system totals to payroll run totals, with variance under 1% documented.
  • Cross-coding is allowed only with written PM justification captured in the audit trail.
  • Labor distribution report is generated monthly and can be produced on demand for any historical month.

03Indirect Cost Pool Structure7 items

SF-1408 Criterion 2c. Indirect costs don't belong to any single contract but still need to be allocated. The government wants to see a logical, consistent method — typically separate pools for fringe benefits, overhead, and general & administrative (G&A) costs.

  • Fringe benefits pool defined and actively accumulating costs (payroll taxes, health insurance, 401k, PTO).
  • G&A pool defined separately from overhead (legal, executive, finance, HR, accounting).
  • Overhead pool defined if you have shared engineering resources beyond fringe and G&A. Many small firms combine overhead into G&A — that's acceptable.
  • Written indirect rate memorandum exists, calculated by a CPA, showing the math behind each provisional rate.
  • Provisional rates applied consistently — you bill at provisional rates throughout the year, reconcile to actuals via the Incurred Cost Submission (ICS).
  • Allocation base is consistent period-over-period — typically direct labor dollars. Changing bases mid-year is a red flag.
  • Indirect rate memo is no older than 12 months and reflects current year's pool dynamics.

04Unallowable Cost Segregation8 items

SF-1408 Criterion 2h. FAR Part 31.205 defines dozens of cost categories that cannot be billed to the government under any circumstance. A cost doesn't need to be intentionally charged to be a problem — if your accountant books team lunches to a direct labor account, you have a finding.

  • Unallowable cost accounts exist in your chart of accounts as dedicated GL buckets separate from billable overhead.
  • Meals and entertainment segregated FAR 31.205-14, 31.205-46 — team lunches, happy hours, client dinners, client coffee.
  • Alcohol expenses segregated FAR 31.205-51 — always unallowable regardless of context.
  • First-class airfare segregated FAR 31.205-46 — coach is the rule, first-class requires written justification.
  • Gifts segregated FAR 31.205-13 — client gifts, holiday gifts, employee gifts all count.
  • Lobbying and political contributions segregated FAR 31.205-22.
  • Written unallowable cost policy exists and is applied consistently month-over-month.
  • Last 12 months of overhead transactions reviewed for misclassified unallowable entries.
The Compounding Risk

An unallowable cost improperly booked to overhead doesn't just inflate that single invoice — it flows into your overhead rate, which gets applied to every cost-reimbursable invoice you send. A $40,000 mistake in one year's overhead pool can become $400,000 in disallowed costs by the time auditors identify the pattern.

05Accounting System Operation7 items

SF-1408 Criteria 2d, 2g, and Section 3. The auditor isn't just evaluating your configuration — they want to see operational evidence. Systems that have been run through monthly close cycles with real data are credible. Configurations that have never been tested are not.

  • Monthly close completes within 10 business days of month-end. Labor distributed, indirect pools accumulated, cost reports available.
  • Labor-to-GL reconciliation procedure is documented and has a named owner who signs off monthly.
  • At least 3 monthly close cycles have been executed with real data before Phase II submission.
  • Test cost voucher has been generated — a practice run showing direct labor + fringe + overhead + G&A in invoice format, even if run against internal overhead.
  • Monthly cost reports by contract can be produced on demand for any historical month.
  • Voucher preparation procedure is written and can be executed by someone other than the accountant who set up the system.
  • Cross-training completed — at least two people in the organization understand the indirect rate structure and can answer auditor questions.

06Phase II Proposal Appendix6 items

The government doesn't require an SF-1408 readiness package with your proposal — but submitting one proactively signals maturity, shortens the pre-award survey timeline by 30-90 days, and moves your award into a faster review lane.

  • Cover memo addressed to the contracting officer identifying your SF-1408 readiness and referencing the specific criteria the appendix addresses.
  • Chart of accounts summary (1-2 pages) showing direct/indirect segregation and unallowable account structure.
  • Indirect rate memorandum (2-4 pages) prepared by your CPA with provisional rate calculations and allocation methodology.
  • Timekeeping policy (1-2 pages) stating daily entry requirements, contract attribution, and review process.
  • Sample labor distribution report from the most recent complete month, redacted if needed, showing hours by employee by contract.
  • SF-1408 self-assessment summary (1 page) showing your internal score against each criterion with remediation plan for any item below 85.

07CPA Partner & External Support4 items

Your accountant handles the books. Your compliance layer handles daily labor capture. You need both, and you need them to work together. Most SBIR teams fail here by assuming one party is handling what the other expects them to handle.

  • GovCon-experienced CPA engaged — not a general small-business CPA. Ask for their current SBIR client count and most recent ICS filing experience.
  • 4-8 working session hours per month during Phase II preparation (month 1-3 post-award is the heavy lift).
  • Clear division of responsibilities documented in writing between CPA (GL, rates, ICS) and compliance platform (labor capture, CLIN mapping, audit trail).
  • Disaster continuity plan — if your CPA leaves mid-Phase II, at least one person internally can walk through every part of the system.
