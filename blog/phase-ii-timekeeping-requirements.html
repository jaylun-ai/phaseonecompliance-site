Phase II Timekeeping Requirements Every SBIR Team Should Know Before They Apply

As a Principal Investigator preparing for a Phase II SBIR application, understanding the intricacies of Phase II timekeeping is crucial. A staggering 70% of SBIR teams report difficulties during the transition from Phase I to Phase II, primarily due to inadequate preparation in timekeeping practices. This can lead to significant delays and complications when the DCAA comes into play. In this post, we’ll explore the essential timekeeping requirements you need to grasp to ensure compliance and a smooth transition.

Understanding the Regulations: What You Need to Know

Phase II contracts are typically cost-reimbursable, governed by the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) Part 31. This means that the government will reimburse costs incurred during the project, but only if they are adequately documented. One critical component of this documentation is the daily labor distribution records, which track how much time each employee spends on the project. The SF-1408 pre-award accounting system survey requires these records to be complete and accurate from day one of Phase I.

Specifically, the SF-1408 survey assesses whether your accounting system can accurately track costs and comply with government regulations. Without a robust system in place, your team may face delays in funding and increased scrutiny during audits.

Common Pitfalls: Where Phase I Teams Fall Short

Many Phase I teams operate under the assumption that the firm fixed-price nature of their contracts means they can afford to be lax with timekeeping. This assumption is misleading. For instance, a team of five engineers working on a $1 million Phase I project may not prioritize daily labor distribution, thinking it unnecessary. However, when they prepare for Phase II, they often find themselves scrambling to recreate records that should have been maintained from the start.

One common scenario involves a team that begins preparing for their Phase II application only three months before the deadline. They realize that they need to provide daily labor distribution records for the entire Phase I period. The result? They spend an additional 2-3 months reconstructing timekeeping records, risking their application timeline and project funding.

Preparation Strategies: What Smart Teams Do Differently

Successful SBIR teams recognize that preparation is key. They start building their audit trail early—ideally within the first few months of Phase I. This means implementing a timekeeping system that captures daily labor distribution accurately and consistently. For example, a team of six engineers might use an automated timekeeping tool that integrates with their project management software, ensuring that every hour worked is logged in real-time.

By adopting this proactive approach, they not only streamline their internal processes but also create a clear and verifiable audit trail that satisfies DCAA requirements. This preparation allows them to submit their Phase II application with confidence, knowing they have the necessary documentation at their fingertips.

Conclusion: The Importance of Early Action

In summary, understanding Phase II timekeeping requirements is essential for any SBIR team looking to transition smoothly from Phase I. By focusing on daily labor distribution and maintaining accurate records from the outset, you can avoid the common pitfalls that lead to delays and complications.
