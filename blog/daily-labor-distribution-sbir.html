Why Phase II Requires Daily Labor Distribution (And How to Build the Habit Early)

In a recent survey of defense tech companies preparing for Phase II SBIR/STTR applications, 78% reported struggling with daily labor distribution records. This is a critical requirement that can significantly impact your project's success. As you transition from Phase I, where contracts are typically firm fixed price, to Phase II, which is cost-reimbursable, the need for precise daily labor distribution becomes paramount.

Understanding the Phase II Requirement

The Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) Part 31 governs the cost principles applicable to government contracts, and it mandates that cost-reimbursable contracts require accurate daily labor distribution records. Specifically, the SF-1408 pre-award accounting system survey assesses your accounting system's ability to allocate direct and indirect costs appropriately. This survey necessitates comprehensive documentation of labor hours, which must be maintained from the outset of your Phase I project.

Failing to establish these records early can lead to significant delays. If your team is unprepared, you may find yourself scrambling to reconstruct labor records just as your Phase II deadline approaches, jeopardizing your funding and project timeline.

Common Pitfalls for Phase I Teams

Many Phase I teams overlook the importance of daily labor distribution, often assuming that their firm fixed price contracts shield them from immediate compliance concerns. For example, a team of five engineers working on a Phase I contract may log their hours casually, without formal tracking. When they apply for Phase II, they realize that their informal records lack the granularity needed for the SF-1408 survey. This oversight can lead to a 2-3 month reconstruction effort, causing stress and potential loss of funding.

Another common shortfall is not distinguishing between direct and indirect costs. If your team does not accurately categorize labor hours, you risk misallocating costs, which can lead to compliance issues during the survey. This can be particularly detrimental for smaller teams, where every dollar counts.

What Smart Teams Do Differently

Successful teams start building their daily labor distribution habit early, often within the first three months of Phase I. They implement an automated timekeeping system that integrates seamlessly with their project management tools, allowing for real-time tracking of labor hours. For instance, a team of six engineers might set up a system that automatically logs hours against specific project tasks, ensuring that they have detailed records ready when they transition to Phase II.

Moreover, these teams conduct regular audits of their timekeeping practices. By reviewing their records monthly, they can identify discrepancies and correct them before they become larger issues. This proactive approach not only streamlines the transition to Phase II but also instills a culture of compliance within the team.
