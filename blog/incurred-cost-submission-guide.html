Incurred Cost Submission Guide for First-Time Government Contractors

Entering the realm of government contracting can be both exciting and daunting, especially for small businesses and first-time contractors. One critical aspect of this journey is understanding the Incurred Cost Submission (ICS) process. This guide aims to demystify the ICS, offering actionable insights for small defense contractors, particularly those who have recently received Phase I Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) awards.

What is Incurred Cost Submission (ICS)?

Incurred Cost Submission refers to the process through which contractors report their actual costs incurred during the performance of a government contract. The Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) requires this submission to ensure that costs are allowable, allocable, and reasonable as per the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) Part 31 guidelines.

Why is ICS Important?

For small contractors, especially those working under government contracts, the ICS serves several crucial functions:

  • Compliance: Ensures adherence to DCAA guidelines and FAR regulations.
  • Financial Recovery: Enables contractors to recover costs incurred while fulfilling contract obligations.
  • Audit Readiness: Prepares your organization for potential audits, ensuring documentation is in order.

Understanding the Regulations

To grasp the ICS process fully, it is essential to understand the relevant regulations:

  • FAR Part 31: This section outlines the principles for determining the allowability of costs. Familiarize yourself with the types of costs that can be claimed under your contract.
  • DCAA Guidelines: These guidelines provide specifics on how to prepare your ICS, including required documentation and reporting formats.
  • SF-1408: This form assesses the adequacy of your accounting system. A positive assessment is crucial for a successful ICS submission.

Steps for Preparing Your Incurred Cost Submission

Here’s a step-by-step guide to help you prepare your ICS:

1. Establish a Compliant Accounting System

Your accounting system must meet DCAA standards. Key components include:

  • Timekeeping: Implement a timekeeping system that accurately tracks employee hours against specific contracts.
  • Cost Allocation: Ensure you can allocate costs to the correct contracts and cost objectives.
  • Documentation: Maintain thorough documentation of all incurred costs.

2. Gather Necessary Documentation

Documentation is critical for a successful ICS submission. Key documents include:

  • Timecards: Records of hours worked by employees on government contracts.
  • Invoices: All invoices related to contract performance.
  • Payroll Records: Detailed payroll records to support labor costs.
  • Subcontractor Information: Documentation for any subcontractors involved in the project.

3. Complete the Incurred Cost Submission

The ICS typically includes the following components:

  • Schedule of Incurred Costs: A detailed breakdown of all costs incurred during the contract period.
  • Overhead Rates: Calculation of overhead rates based on incurred costs.
  • Indirect Rates: Documentation supporting any indirect rates claimed.

4. Submit Your ICS

Once your ICS is complete, submit it to the DCAA. Ensure that you meet the submission deadline, usually within six months after the end of your fiscal year. Failure to submit on time can result in significant penalties.

Real-World Scenarios

Scenario 1: The Importance of Accurate Timekeeping

A small defense contractor, Tech Innovations LLC, faced challenges during their first ICS submission. Their timekeeping system was outdated, leading to discrepancies in reported hours. As a result, the DCAA flagged their submission for audit. By investing in a modern timekeeping solution that integrated seamlessly with their accounting system, they improved accuracy and compliance, ultimately recovering significant costs in their next submission.

Scenario 2: The Need for Comprehensive Documentation

Another contractor, Secure Solutions Inc., learned the hard way about the importance of documentation. During an audit, they were unable to provide sufficient evidence of incurred costs, resulting in disallowed expenses. They revamped their documentation processes, implementing a rigorous system for tracking all invoices and timecards. This proactive approach not only facilitated their ICS submission but also instilled confidence in their financial practices.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

First-time contractors often make several common mistakes when submitting their ICS. Here are a few to watch out for:

  • Inadequate Documentation: Failing to maintain proper records can lead to disallowed costs.
  • Missing Deadlines: Always be aware of submission deadlines to avoid penalties.
  • Neglecting to Update Rates: Ensure your indirect and overhead rates are updated regularly to reflect actual costs.

Conclusion

Understanding the Incurred Cost Submission process is crucial for first-time government contractors. By establishing a compliant accounting system, gathering necessary documentation, and avoiding common pitfalls, you can streamline your ICS and ensure compliance with DCAA regulations. Remember, investing time and resources into this process can lead to significant financial recovery and better audit readiness.
