FAR Part 31 Cost Principles Explained for Small Businesses

As a small defense contractor, navigating the complexities of federal regulations can be daunting, especially when it comes to understanding FAR Part 31 and its associated cost principles. These principles are crucial for ensuring that your costs are allowable, allocable, and reasonable, which is essential for maintaining compliance with federal contracts. In this blog post, we'll break down these cost principles and provide actionable advice to help your small business thrive in the competitive landscape of government contracting.

Understanding FAR Part 31

The Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) is a set of rules governing the acquisition process by which federal agencies procure goods and services. FAR Part 31 specifically addresses the cost principles applicable to contracts with the federal government. These principles are designed to ensure that costs claimed by contractors are justified and consistent with the terms of the contract.

Key Cost Principles Under FAR Part 31

FAR Part 31 outlines several key principles that small businesses must adhere to when accounting for costs. Understanding these principles is vital for ensuring compliance and avoiding potential audit issues. Here are the main categories:

  • Allowable Costs: Costs that can be reimbursed under a federal contract. These must be necessary, reasonable, and allocable to the contract.
  • Unallowable Costs: Costs that cannot be charged to federal contracts. Examples include entertainment expenses and lobbying costs.
  • Allocable Costs: Costs that can be directly tied to a specific contract or project. These costs must benefit the contract in question.
  • Reasonable Costs: Costs that a prudent person would incur under similar circumstances. This includes ensuring that costs do not exceed what is generally accepted in the marketplace.

Allowable Costs: A Deeper Dive

To ensure compliance, it’s essential to understand what constitutes allowable costs. According to FAR 31.201-2, costs must meet the following criteria:

  • Necessary for the performance of the contract: The cost must be directly related to fulfilling the terms of the contract.
  • Reasonable: Costs must not exceed what is generally accepted as ordinary and necessary.
  • Allocable: Costs must be assignable to a specific contract based on the benefits received.

Common Allowable Costs for Small Businesses

For small businesses, understanding which costs are allowable can significantly impact your bottom line. Common allowable costs include:

  • Direct Labor: Salaries and wages of employees working directly on the contract.
  • Materials and Supplies: Items purchased specifically for contract performance.
  • Overhead Costs: Indirect costs that support the contract, such as utilities and office supplies.
  • Travel Expenses: Costs incurred for travel that is necessary for contract performance.

Real-World Scenario: Navigating Allowable Costs

Consider a small business that has received a Phase I SBIR award to develop a new defense technology. The team spends significant time on research and development, which is allowable under FAR Part 31. However, they also take their clients to a local restaurant to discuss project updates. While the research expenses are allowable, the restaurant bill would be considered an unallowable cost. This distinction is crucial for maintaining compliance and ensuring that the business can recover its costs.

Unallowable Costs: What to Avoid

Understanding unallowable costs is just as important as knowing allowable costs. FAR 31.201-5 specifies several categories of unallowable costs, including:

  • Entertainment Expenses: Costs related to social activities, including meals and events.
  • Lobbying Costs: Expenses incurred to influence legislation or government policy.
  • Fines and Penalties: Costs resulting from violations of laws or regulations.

Real-World Scenario: The Cost of Non-Compliance

Imagine a small defense contractor that mistakenly includes lobbying costs in their proposal for a federal contract. During an audit, the Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) flags these costs as unallowable, resulting in a significant reduction in reimbursable expenses. This scenario highlights the importance of understanding unallowable costs to avoid financial penalties and compliance issues.

Best Practices for Small Businesses

To ensure compliance with FAR Part 31 and effectively manage costs, small businesses can implement the following best practices:

  • Maintain Accurate Records: Keep detailed records of all costs incurred, including invoices, receipts, and timekeeping logs.
  • Regular Training: Provide training for your team on allowable and unallowable costs to ensure everyone understands compliance requirements.
  • Implement a Robust Accounting System: Use accounting software that can track costs in real-time and flag unallowable expenses.
  • Consult DCAA Guidelines: Regularly review DCAA guidelines and updates to stay informed about changes in compliance requirements.

Conclusion

Understanding FAR Part 31 and its cost principles is essential for small businesses engaged in federal contracting. By focusing on allowable costs and avoiding unallowable expenses, you can enhance your compliance efforts and position your business for success in the competitive landscape of government contracts. Implementing best practices for cost management will not only help you stay compliant but also improve your financial health.
